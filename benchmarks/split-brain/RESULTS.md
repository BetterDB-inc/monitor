# Split-brain detection by node coverage: Stage 1 (6 nodes)

Tables are generated by `pnpm bench report` from the recorded trials. The findings and limitations are written by hand from those tables.

## Findings

All numbers are for product commit `bfbfa505`, Valkey 9.1.3, 6 nodes and a node timeout of 5 s. 390 trials were recorded, 30 per scenario. Section numbers point at the tables below.

1. **No control raised an event.** None of the 210 control trials raised an event at any coverage, any N, either monitor position or any join variant (section 6). Per scenario that is 0 of 30, with a 95% upper bound of 11% per trial; pooled over the seven different operations it is 0 of 210, upper bound 1.8%. The views of two nodes did differ during a clean join (29 of 30 trials), a legacy reshard (20 of 30) and an atomic migration (5 of 30), but never for longer than 4 s, 2 s and 1 s (section 2). On false positives N = 2, N = 3 and N = 5 are a tie: all three are zero.

2. **Both partitions were raised by every setting when the monitor could reach every node.** Zone partition and minority partition: 100% [89–100] at every coverage and every N (section 1). Coverage changed only the time (section 12). Against what ships today (zone 1, N = 3, median 18–19 s after the cut), k=1 was 3.8 s later in the minority partition and 5.3 s later in the zone partition, and k=2 was 0.9 s and 1.6 s later. k=3 and zone 2 were within 0.2 s. k=4, k=5 and all nodes were 1.1–1.5 s sooner in the minority partition and a tie in the zone partition.

3. **Each round of persistence cost 5 s and bought nothing measurable here.** With the same coverage, N = 2 raised 5.0 s sooner than N = 3 and N = 5 raised 10.0 s later, in every trial. Raise rates were the same in every trial (section 12). Events closed 8 s, 13 s and 23 s after the heal at N = 2, 3 and 5 (section 8).

4. **A monitor on one side of the cut raised nothing, at any coverage.** When the monitor can read only the nodes on the seed's side, both partitions gave 0% at every coverage, asking all nodes included (section 4): the nodes it can still read agree with each other. More coverage does not fix this. On the same recordings the seed's own `cluster_state` turned to fail for every cut-off seed (60 of 60) and for 64 of 120 (minority partition) and 9 of 120 (zone partition) seeds on the larger side, 7–8 s after the cut (section 9).

5. **Killed primary, paused node and flapping node were never raised, and no coverage could have raised them.** 0 of 30 at every setting (section 1). The nodes' views disagreed for at most 2 s, 2 s and 5 s in these scenarios, with medians of 1 s, 1 s and 3 s (section 2); an event at N = 2 needs a disagreement at two rounds 5 s apart. At a 5 s node timeout the cluster agrees that the node failed faster than the detector's round interval. This is not a coverage miss. A 15 s node timeout was not measured.

6. **Coverage mattered in one scenario: the failure-flag dispute of the partial join.** In all 30 trials every node listed the joiner within 1.9–4.9 s, and then the three nodes cut off from it kept it flagged as failed while the other three did not, for the rest of the 150 s window (141–145 s), ending within 1 s of the heal. Raise rates (section 1): k=1 57% [54–60], k=2 79% [72–84], k=3 90% [83–96], zone 1 89% [82–96], and 100% [89–100] for k=4, k=5, zone 2 and all nodes. zone 1 and k=3 are a tie. k=4, k=5, zone 2 and all nodes are 10.6 points [4.4, 17.8] above zone 1 and 3.0–5.8 s sooner (section 12). Counting only membership after 90 s raised nothing (section 10), because membership was never in dispute for that long.

7. **k=1 follows the accused.** It asks no second node until the seed's own view flags someone, so it costs no extra read while idle and about one extra read per round during a fault (section 11). That is why it is 4–5 s later on the partitions and misses 43% of the partial-join replays, and why it sees almost nothing from inside a cut (7% on the partial join, section 4).

8. **zone 2 is not full fan-out at 6 nodes.** 2 per zone gives primaries only when every zone has a primary. Here every zone has one, so zone 2 compared 3 views per round when the seed was a primary and 4 when it was a replica (3.5 on average), against 3 for zone 1 and 6 for all nodes (section 11). Asking all nodes is its own row: 5 extra reads and 3.7 KB per round, 60 extra reads a minute, against 2 extra reads and 1.5 KB for zone 1.

9. **The single-view signals are a different kind of signal, and one of them fired in a control.** The duplicate-primary signal fired in no scenario. The cluster-state signal fired in the rolling restart: each restarted primary reported its own state as fail for 2–3 s after it came back, in 30 of 30 trials (section 9). The divergence detector raised nothing there.

What Stage 1 does not settle: at 6 nodes zone 1 and k=3 pick nearly the same nodes, no control disagreed for as long as one round, and the three short faults never disagreed long enough for any setting to raise. So these recordings do not separate N = 2 from N = 5 on false positives, and they separate coverages only on the partial join. 12 and 30 nodes and a 15 s node timeout are not measured.

Recording quality (section 13): 12 attempts were dropped because host load crossed the limit and were recorded again; no kept trial had a p99 tick lateness over 15 ms; the shipped setting raised no event before the injection in any trial.

## How to read the tables

- **k=1 … k=5**: the product setting `randomNodes` with zones hidden from the detector. It is the size of the sample including the seed node, so k=1 asks no extra node unless the seed itself accuses someone.
- **zone 1, zone 2**: the product setting `nodesPerZone` with `randomNodes` 3 and zones known. **zone 1 with N = 3 is what ships today.**
- **all**: every node is asked every round.
- **N**: rounds a disagreement must last before an event is raised (`minRounds`).
- A cell such as `97% [91–100]` is the mean over trials of the share of replays that raised an event, with a 95% interval. The interval is a bootstrap over trials, and it is never narrower than the Wilson interval for the number of trials that raised at all, so one raising trial of 30 gives an upper end near 17%, and none of 30 gives 11%.
- In the control tables a cell such as `1/30 [1–17], 0% of replays` is the number of trials in which at least one replay raised an event, with its Wilson interval, then the share of all replays that raised.
- A time such as `12 s [11 s–14 s], p90 16 s, never 3/30` is the median over trials of the per-trial median, with a bootstrap 95% interval of that median, the 90th percentile over trials, and the number of trials in which no replay raised an event. Trials that never raised are not in the time. Times are counted from the moment the fault was injected.
- Fault windows run from the injection to the heal, or to the end of the recording when the fault is never healed. Control windows run from the start of the operation to the end of the recording.

## Limitations

- **One cluster size.** Stage 1 is 6 nodes only. With 3 primaries in 3 zones, "zone 1" and "k=3" pick almost the same nodes, and "zone 2" cannot reach 2 per zone because a zone's lane only offers its primaries. Coverage differences should be read again at 12 and 30 nodes (Stage 2). At 30 nodes "all" is also capped by the detector's limit of 12 observers per round.
- **Node timeout is 5 s, not the 15 s default.** It keeps a trial short. Fault timing scales with the node timeout, the 5 s round interval does not, so times to the first event and the effect of N will differ at 15 s.
- **The service glue is restated.** The detector modules are the real ones, but the code that turns a round into raised and closed events is a copy of the logic in `anomaly.service.ts`. It passes the 12 service test cases it can express. It does not model: storage refusing a close, an event dismissed by hand, two connections or an agent watching the same cluster, hand-over between connections, adopting stored events after a restart, Raft clusters, the feature being switched off, and connection removal. The service files it was copied from are in the file pin, so a change there stops the replay until the copy is checked again.
- **One sample per second.** The product reads the views of a round a few milliseconds apart; the replay uses views taken within the same second. Replay uses the planned time of each sample; how late a sample really started is recorded and shown in section 13. Anything shorter than a second is invisible, including migration jobs that start and finish between two samples.
- **Rounds are replayed, not timed.** A read that failed in the recording (2 s limit, the product's fan-out limit) fails in the replay. Slow but successful reads do not delay the next round as they could in the product.
- **Monitor position is modelled, not run.** "Outside" uses what the recorder on the Docker host saw; in the four scenarios that cut the network it had no failed read. "Inside" removes the reads that cross a cut the seed node is on. No monitor process ran inside the cluster network.
- **Local, small and fast.** All nodes share one machine and one bridge network. Gossip is faster and steadier than across real zones. The migration controls hold 800k keys of 256 bytes. An atomic move of 1000 slots (about 49k keys) takes 0.2 s here, so the atomic migration control runs under a rate limit: the primaries' links are held to 20 Mbit/s for the whole operation, which stretches a move to 6–7 s. The limit applies to everything those nodes send, the cluster bus included, so an event in that control can come from the limit and not from the migration; section 6 lists how many control trials had any view difference next to how many raised an event. A move that did not end as `success` is not in the results; the attempt was dropped and recorded again. The legacy reshard moves 3000 slots in about 9 s, where the design said 500, because 500 slots took 2 s and would not span a 5 s round. Restarts take one to three seconds. The controls are gentler than production operations.
- **30 trials bound the rare cases loosely.** A setting that raised nothing in 30 trials still has a 95% upper bound of about 11% per trial.
- **Seed-only is a different kind of signal.** The seed-only table shows today's single-view detectors on the same recordings. It is not the divergence detector with fewer nodes and its rates are not comparable cell by cell.
- **Fault windows are judged by the recording, not by a person.** A killed primary, a long pause and a flap that ends in a failover all end with the cluster agreeing again. The tables report whether an event was raised while the views differed; they do not say an event should have been raised.
- **Partial join did not produce a partially known node.** The joiner is cut off from half of the nodes and introduced to one of the others. At 6 nodes every node listed the joiner within 5 s through gossip, so the lasting state was a dispute over the joiner's failure flag, not over membership. The partially known node of issue #691 is not measured here. Section 10 shows the same recordings with membership counted on its own after 90 s, which is how a separate partial-membership event would see them.
