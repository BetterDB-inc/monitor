## Limitations

- **One cluster size.** Stage 1 is 6 nodes only. With 3 primaries in 3 zones, "zone 1" and "k=3" pick almost the same nodes, and "zone 2" cannot reach 2 per zone because a zone's lane only offers its primaries. Coverage differences should be read again at 12 and 30 nodes (Stage 2). At 30 nodes "all" is also capped by the detector's limit of 12 observers per round.
- **Node timeout is 5 s, not the 15 s default.** It keeps a trial short. Fault timing scales with the node timeout, the 5 s round interval does not, so times to the first event and the effect of N will differ at 15 s.
- **The service glue is restated.** The detector modules are the real ones, but the code that turns a round into raised and closed events is a copy of the logic in `anomaly.service.ts`. It passes the 12 service test cases it can express. It does not model: storage refusing a close, an event dismissed by hand, two connections or an agent watching the same cluster, hand-over between connections, adopting stored events after a restart, Raft clusters, the feature being switched off, and connection removal. The service files it was copied from are in the file pin, so a change there stops the replay until the copy is checked again.
- **One sample per second.** The product reads the views of a round a few milliseconds apart; the replay uses views taken within the same second. Replay uses the planned time of each sample; how late a sample really started is recorded and shown in section 13. Anything shorter than a second can be missed, such as a migration job that starts and finishes between two samples. A run of n samples that differ was seen over n − 1 s; it lasted less than n + 1 s only when it starts and ends inside the window.
- **Rounds are replayed, not timed.** A read that failed in the recording (2 s limit, the product's fan-out limit) fails in the replay. Slow but successful reads do not delay the next round as they could in the product.
- **Monitor position is modelled, not run.** "Outside" uses what the recorder on the Docker host saw; in the four scenarios that cut the network it had no failed read. "Inside" removes the reads that cross a cut the seed node is on. No monitor process ran inside the cluster network.
- **Local, small and fast.** All nodes share one machine and one bridge network. Gossip is faster and steadier than across real zones. The migration controls hold 800k keys of 256 bytes. An atomic move of 1000 slots (about 49k keys) took 0.2 s in a pilot run, so the atomic migration control runs under a rate limit: the primaries' links are held to 20 Mbit/s for the whole operation. Under the limit the 90 recorded moves took 6.0–7.3 s each. The limit applies to everything those nodes send, the cluster bus included, so an event in that control can come from the limit and not from the migration; section 6 lists how many control trials had any view difference next to how many raised an event. A move that did not end as `success` is not in the results; the attempt would be dropped and recorded again, which did not happen in Stage 1. The legacy reshard asks for 3000 slots; `valkey-cli` moved 3000 in 9 trials and 3001 in 21 (139k–161k keys) and took 7.5–10.2 s. The design said 500 slots; in a pilot run 500 slots took 2 s, which would not span a 5 s round. A restart took 0.7–3.9 s. The controls are gentler than production operations.
- **Stage 1 was recorded before three fixes to the recording.** (1) A sample that ran into the 2 s limit closed the connection that the next sample to the same node was waiting on, so that sample failed early. It happens only while a node accepts a connection and does not answer, which in Stage 1 is the paused node: 277 of its 428 timed-out samples were cut short, and in all 30 trials the first answer after the pause follows one of them. The paused node may therefore have answered up to 1 s earlier than recorded. Counted again from the raw recordings with the node's first answer after the pause also placed one sample earlier, where it differs from the other nodes in all 30 trials, the most samples in a row that differed is 3 instead of the 2 in section 2, and still no trial has views that differ at two rounds in a row (0 of 30, rounds placed as in section 2). That recount is not in the tables. Samples of the other nodes, and failed samples of a killed or restarting node, which are refused at once, were not affected. (2) The legacy reshard is now dropped when it reports an error or moves fewer slots than asked; in the 30 recorded trials the source gave up 3000 or 3001 slots. (3) A rolling restart is now dropped when the cluster does not agree again within 60 s of a restart; all 180 recorded restarts did. None of the three changes the recorded trials, and the paused node was not recorded again.
- **30 trials bound the rare cases loosely.** A setting that raised nothing in 30 trials still has a 95% upper bound of about 11% per trial.
- **Seed-only is a different kind of signal.** The seed-only table shows today's single-view detectors on the same recordings. It is not the divergence detector with fewer nodes and its rates are not comparable cell by cell.
- **Fault windows are judged by the recording, not by a person.** A killed primary, a long pause and a flap that ends in a failover all end with the cluster agreeing again. The tables report whether an event was raised while the views differed; they do not say an event should have been raised.
- **Partial join did not produce a partially known node.** The joiner is cut off from half of the nodes and introduced to one of the others. At 6 nodes every node listed the joiner within 5 s through gossip, so the lasting state was a dispute over the joiner's failure flag, not over membership. The partially known node of issue #691 is not measured here. Section 10 shows the same recordings with membership counted on its own after 90 s, which is how a separate partial-membership event would see them.
